## Democratic Republic of the Congo

As of 24 November 2026, the Ministry of Health has recorded 12,021 confirmed cases, including 5,904 deaths, since the outbreak was declared on 15 May. 3,332 people have recovered.[[drcSitrep194]]

- **This week (17 to 24 November):** 408 new confirmed cases and 226 deaths were added to the totals of 17 November (11,613 cases and 5,678 deaths).[[drcSitrep194]][[drcSitrep187]]
- **Where:** Ituri remains the most affected province, with about two thirds of all cases since May. In the three weeks to 17 November, new cases fell by 13% in Ituri but rose by 8% in North Kivu, which reported the most new cases in the last week of that period. Eight provinces and 69 of the country's 178 health zones have reported cases since the start.[[drcSitrep194]][[whoDon630]]
- **Deaths outside treatment centres:** of the 720 deaths reported in the three weeks to 17 November, 452 happened in the community rather than in a treatment centre. WHO notes that this is especially the case for young children, and that it shows illness is often recognised late and people reach care late.[[whoDon630]]
- **Contacts:** on 24 November, 85% of the people being followed as contacts of a case were seen, below the 95% target.[[drcSitrep194]][[whoDon630]]
- **Vaccination:** Ervebo, the vaccine approved against Ebola virus, is being given to people at high risk and front-line workers, alongside research into whether it protects against Bundibugyo virus. By 24 November, 15,228 people had been vaccinated in Tshopo, 5,342 in North Kivu and 4,740 in Haut-Uélé; vaccination continues in Bunia and Mongbwalu, in Ituri.[[drcSitrep194]]
- **The response:** according to WHO, the response has grown considerably in six months, but rising insecurity is the greatest obstacle to expanding it further.[[whoDon630]]

## Kenya and Nigeria

Kenya has recorded nine confirmed cases, including two deaths, all linked to the first imported case. No new case has been reported since 19 October. All seven people treated in hospital have recovered: the last left hospital on 11 November, and Kenya then began a 42-day countdown, twice the longest time it takes for symptoms to appear, before it can declare its outbreak over.[[whoDon630]]

Nigeria's only case, reported on 20 October, was moved to Geneva, Switzerland, for treatment on 26 October, recovered and left hospital on 11 November. All 62 people identified as contacts in Nigeria completed their 21 days of follow-up, and Nigeria is also counting down 42 days.[[whoDon630]]

## Other countries

According to WHO, the outbreaks in France and Uganda have been under control since August. On 18 November, the United Kingdom reported that a health worker brought back from the Democratic Republic of the Congo for treatment had recovered and left hospital.[[whoDon630]]

## WHO's assessment

- **International emergency:** on 17 November, WHO's Emergency Committee met for the third time. On 18 November, following its advice, WHO decided that the outbreak remains a public health emergency of international concern. Updated recommendations for countries will follow.[[whoDon630]]
- **Risk:** WHO judges the risk very high in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, the only country where the virus is spreading in the community. It is high in Kenya and Nigeria and in other countries with close links to affected areas and a weaker capacity to respond, and low elsewhere.[[whoDon630]]
- **Travel:** WHO continues to advise against any restriction of travel to, or trade with, affected countries.[[whoDon630]]

## What this means for you

- If you or someone near you has symptoms, contact your national health hotline straight away: see the [helplines](../../help/).
- If you have been in contact with a sick person, see [what to do and the 21 days of follow-up](../../health/contacts/).
- If someone dies and Ebola is possible, do not touch the body: see [safe and dignified burials](../../health/safe-burials/).
