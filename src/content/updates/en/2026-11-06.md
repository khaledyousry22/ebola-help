## Democratic Republic of the Congo

As of 3 November 2026, the Ministry of Health has recorded 10,668 confirmed cases, including 5,198 deaths, since the outbreak was declared on 15 May. 2,881 people have recovered.

- **This week (28 October to 3 November):** 475 new confirmed cases and 238 deaths, slightly fewer than the week before (489 cases and 273 deaths).
- **Where:** Ituri remains the most affected province, with about 7 in 10 of all cases since May. On 3 November, North Kivu reported more new cases than Ituri (36 against 23). Eight provinces and 67 of the country's 178 health zones have reported cases since the start.
- **Contacts:** health teams were following almost 40,000 people who had been in contact with a case. On 3 November, 84% of them were seen, below the 95% target. In Bas-Uélé, all contacts have completed their 21 days of follow-up.
- **Deaths outside treatment centres:** many deaths still happen in the community rather than in treatment centres (18 of the 34 deaths reported on 3 November). WHO says this shows that illness is often recognised late and people reach care late.
- **Vaccination:** the Ervebo vaccine is being given as part of research into whether it protects against Bundibugyo virus: 2,699 people have been vaccinated in Ituri and 1,617 in North Kivu so far.

## Kenya

According to Africa CDC, Kenya has recorded nine confirmed cases, including two deaths, all linked to the first imported case confirmed on 6 October. No new case has been reported since 19 October, and there is no evidence of sustained spread in the community. On 3 November, five patients were discharged after negative tests, and 46 people had completed their 21 days of follow-up.

Saudi Arabia has introduced Ebola-related travel restrictions affecting Kenya, and Africa CDC has called for them to be lifted. WHO advises against any restriction of travel to, or trade with, affected countries.

## Nigeria and France

Nigeria's one confirmed case, reported on 20 October in Abuja in a woman who had travelled from Uganda, was still its only case on 3 November. WHO also lists France among the five countries that have reported a case since May.

## Vaccines and treatments

- **The TUMAINI trial** is under way in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. It offers vaccination to people who have been in contact with someone with confirmed Bundibugyo virus disease, an approach called ring vaccination. Four vaccination rings were in place by 4 November. The first vaccine tested is Ervebo, approved against Ebola virus, to find out whether it also protects against Bundibugyo virus.
- **New vaccines:** on 4 November, WHO announced that its experts had recommended adding two vaccines designed specifically against Bundibugyo virus: ChAdOx1, developed by the University of Oxford with the Serum Institute of India, and Moderna's mRNA-1469. They will join the trial once national approvals are given. According to WHO, this is the first time vaccines designed against this virus will be tested during an outbreak. Results are expected in early 2027.
- **Medicines:** the PARTNERS trial, running since July, is testing whether MBP134 and remdesivir improve survival, and maftivimab will soon be added. Another study is testing whether obeldesivir can prevent illness in people who have been exposed to the virus. WHO expects first results from the treatment studies in the coming months.

Until these results are known, there is still no approved vaccine or medicine against Bundibugyo virus. Early supportive care at a treatment centre remains what improves the chance of survival. See [Vaccines and treatment](../../health/vaccines-treatment/).

## What this means for you

- If you or someone near you has symptoms, contact your national health hotline straight away: see the [helplines](../../help/).
- If you have been in contact with a sick person, see [what to do and the 21 days of follow-up](../../health/contacts/).
- If someone dies and Ebola is possible, do not touch the body: see [safe and dignified burials](../../health/safe-burials/).
