## Myth: Ebola spreads through the air, like flu or COVID-19.

**Fact:** Ebola spreads through direct contact with the blood or body fluids of a person who is sick with Ebola or has died of it. You cannot get it simply by being near someone.

## Myth: Mosquitoes can spread Ebola.

**Fact:** There is no evidence that mosquitoes or other insects spread Ebola.

## Myth: People can spread Ebola before they feel ill.

**Fact:** People cannot pass on Ebola before they have symptoms.

## Myth: Everyone who gets Ebola dies.

**Fact:** Many people survive. In past outbreaks, on average about half of the people with Ebola disease survived. Early care at a treatment centre improves the chance of survival.

## Myth: It is safer to care for a sick relative at home.

**Fact:** WHO advises against caring for people with Ebola at home. Treatment centres give care that improves survival and protects families from infection.

## Myth: Salt water, herbs or other home remedies can prevent or cure Ebola.

**Fact:** No herbal or home remedy is approved to prevent or treat Ebola disease. The care that improves survival is given at treatment centres. For Bundibugyo virus, possible medicines are being tested in a WHO-sponsored trial.

## Myth: The vaccine can give you Ebola.

**Fact:** The approved vaccine contains only one gene from Ebola virus, not the whole virus. It cannot cause Ebola disease.

## Myth: Survivors are dangerous and should be kept away.

**Fact:** Survivors whose blood has tested negative do not need to be isolated and should be treated with respect. Because the virus can stay in semen for some time, male survivors are advised to use safer sex practices until tests show the virus has gone, or for 12 months if testing is not available.

## Myth: Ebola is not real.

**Fact:** Ebola disease is confirmed by laboratory tests. In the current outbreak, laboratories in the Democratic Republic of the Congo have confirmed the virus in thousands of people.
