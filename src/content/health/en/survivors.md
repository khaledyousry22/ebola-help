## Recovery is possible

Many people survive Ebola disease. Early supportive care at a treatment centre improves the chance of recovery. In the current outbreak, people are recovering even though no medicine has yet been approved for Bundibugyo virus disease.

## Respect and support

WHO asks that all survivors, their partners and their families be shown respect, dignity and compassion.

Once a survivor's blood has tested negative for the virus, WHO does not recommend keeping them isolated. Having survived Ebola is not a reason to be kept apart from others.

WHO encourages affected countries to set up care programmes for survivors, offering treatment for after-effects, support to return to community life, counselling and testing.

## Health problems after recovery

Some survivors have health problems that last after they recover, both physical and emotional. Survivors should be offered follow-up care. If you are a survivor and have new or lasting health problems, see a health worker.

In rare cases, the illness has come back in a person who had recovered, without a new infection.

## The virus can remain in some parts of the body

In some people who have recovered, the virus can stay for a time in parts of the body such as the testicles, the inside of the eye and the brain.

**For men:** passing on Ebola virus through semen has been documented up to 15 months after recovery, although this is rare. WHO advises:

- where a semen testing programme exists: counselling for male survivors and their partners, safer sex (including condoms) and good hygiene, and a semen test every month until two tests in a row are negative. After that, normal sexual life can resume;
- where there is no testing programme: safer sex for 12 months after recovery.

**For women:** in women infected during pregnancy, the virus can remain in the placenta, the amniotic fluid and the baby. It can also be present in the breast milk of women infected while breastfeeding. If you were pregnant or breastfeeding when you had Ebola disease, ask a health worker for advice. Survivor care programmes should include care for these women.

## Where to find support

Ask the health authorities or your national health hotline about care programmes for survivors in your area. You can find official helplines on our [helplines page](../../help/).
