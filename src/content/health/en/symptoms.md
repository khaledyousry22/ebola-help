## When symptoms start

The time between infection and the first symptoms is between 2 and 21 days. On average, people start to feel ill 8 to 10 days after they were exposed to the virus. A person cannot pass on Ebola before symptoms start.

## Early symptoms

Symptoms often begin suddenly. They can include:

- fever
- great tiredness and weakness
- muscle and joint pain
- headache
- sore throat
- loss of appetite

## Later symptoms

After a few days, the illness can progress to:

- vomiting and diarrhoea
- stomach pain
- skin rash
- signs that the kidneys and liver are not working well

Some people have bleeding, for example from the gums or nose, or blood in vomit or stools. Bleeding is less common than many people think and usually happens later in the illness. Some people also become confused or irritable.

## It can look like other illnesses

In its early stage, Ebola can look like malaria, typhoid fever, flu, meningitis or other infections. Only a laboratory test can tell them apart. This is why it is important to get tested early.

## What to do if you have symptoms

If you or someone near you has these symptoms, especially after contact with a sick person or with someone who has died:

- contact your national health hotline or the local health authorities immediately;
- avoid touching the sick person's blood, vomit, stools or other body fluids;
- do not try to care for the person at home: WHO advises that people with symptoms get care at a health facility.

Early care improves the chance of survival and protects your family and community.

## After recovery

Some people who survive Ebola have lasting health problems, such as tiredness, headaches, muscle and joint pain, or eye and vision problems. Survivors should be offered follow-up care.
