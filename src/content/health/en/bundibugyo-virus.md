## One of three Ebola viruses that cause large outbreaks

Ebola disease can be caused by several related viruses. Three of them are known to cause large outbreaks:

- Ebola virus, which causes Ebola virus disease;
- Sudan virus, which causes Sudan virus disease;
- Bundibugyo virus, which causes Bundibugyo virus disease.

Bundibugyo virus disease is a severe form of Ebola disease. Fruit bats are thought to be the natural host of the virus.

## How it spreads

Like other Ebola diseases, it spreads from person to person through direct contact with the blood or other body fluids of a sick person, or with objects and surfaces soiled with them. The body of a person who has died of the disease can still pass on the virus. According to WHO, the virus spreads more in health facilities where infection prevention measures are not fully applied, and during burials that involve touching the body. More in [How Ebola spreads](../transmission/).

Symptoms appear 2 to 21 days after infection. A person cannot pass on the virus before symptoms start.

## Earlier outbreaks

Before 2026, Bundibugyo virus had caused two known outbreaks: in Uganda in 2007, where about 3 in 10 people with the disease died, and in the Democratic Republic of the Congo in 2012, where about half died.

## The current outbreak

- **Start:** the outbreak was declared in the Democratic Republic of the Congo on 15 May 2026. On 17 May 2026, WHO declared it a public health emergency of international concern.
- **Size:** according to WHO, it is the largest Ebola disease outbreak ever recorded in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Cases are concentrated in the east of the country, with Ituri and North Kivu the most affected provinces in October 2026.
- **Uganda** reported 20 confirmed cases, most of them in people who had come from the Democratic Republic of the Congo. Its outbreak was declared over on 27 August 2026, after 42 days without a new case: twice the longest time it takes for symptoms to appear.
- **Imported cases:** in October 2026, Kenya confirmed a case in a person who had travelled from the Democratic Republic of the Congo through Uganda, and Nigeria confirmed a case in a woman who had recently travelled to Uganda.
- **Risk:** in its assessment of August 2026, WHO judged the risk very high in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, high in neighbouring countries, and low in the rest of Africa and the world.
- **Travel:** WHO advises against restricting travel to, or trade with, affected countries, and does not recommend closing borders or suspending flights.

## Vaccines and medicines

There is no approved vaccine or medicine for Bundibugyo virus disease. Those that exist work against Ebola virus only. Research is under way during the outbreak:

- **Vaccines:** in July 2026, a WHO expert group recommended testing Ervebo, the vaccine approved against Ebola virus, in this outbreak. On 23 October, the TUMAINI trial began in Bunia. It offers vaccination to people who have been in contact with a confirmed case, an approach called ring vaccination, starting with Ervebo. On 4 November, WHO experts recommended adding the first two vaccines designed specifically against Bundibugyo virus, ChAdOx1 and mRNA-1469, once national approvals are given. Results are expected in early 2027.
- **Medicines:** since 2 July 2026, a clinical trial sponsored by WHO, called PARTNERS, has been testing whether an antibody medicine (MBP134) and an antiviral medicine (remdesivir), alone or together, improve survival. Another antibody medicine, maftivimab, will soon be added. A separate study is testing whether obeldesivir can prevent illness in people who have been exposed to the virus.

More in [Vaccines and treatment](../vaccines-treatment/).

## What makes the difference

Early care saves lives. Fluids, nutrition and treatment of symptoms at a treatment centre improve the chance of survival. WHO reports that many deaths in this outbreak happen in the community, outside treatment centres, which points to delays in recognising the illness and reaching care.

If you or someone near you has symptoms, contact your national health hotline or the local health authorities straight away.
