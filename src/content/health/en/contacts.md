## Who counts as a contact

You may be at risk if you had direct physical contact with a person who is sick with, or has died of, suspected or confirmed Ebola disease. This includes touching their blood or other body fluids, such as vomit, stools, sweat or saliva, or objects soiled with them, such as bedding and clothes.

According to WHO, the people most at risk during an outbreak are:

- health and care workers in close contact with patients;
- family members and others who care for a sick person or touch them;
- mourners who touch the body during funerals or burial rituals.

## What to do

Contact a health worker, your local health facility or your national health hotline. Explain what kind of contact you had and when. They will advise you on the next steps.

You can find official helplines on our [helplines page](../../help/).

## The 21 days of follow-up

Once you have been identified as a contact, health workers will follow your health for 21 days after the contact. This is because symptoms can appear up to 21 days after infection. You will be encouraged to:

- accept a daily visit from the contact tracing team;
- let them take your temperature;
- answer their questions as accurately as you can, and ask any questions you have;
- report any symptoms as soon as they start;
- avoid travelling unless you have discussed it with your local health authority;
- accept a vaccine if one is offered to you as part of the response.

You may also be asked to help identify the people you have been in contact with.

This follow-up protects you: if you fall ill, you can get care early, which improves the chance of survival and protects your family.

## If symptoms start

Symptoms often begin suddenly, with fever, great tiredness, muscle pain, headache or sore throat. See [Symptoms](../symptoms/).

Tell the follow-up team or your health worker immediately. You will be advised to go to a care centre, where a laboratory test will show whether you have Ebola disease. Early care improves survival.

## Your family and neighbours

A person cannot pass on Ebola before symptoms start. While you have no symptoms, you are not a risk to the people around you. Being a contact does not mean you are ill.

## Travel

WHO does not recommend lockdowns or quarantines of areas affected by Ebola. But people who have been in contact with a case should avoid or postpone travel where possible, to protect others and to make sure their follow-up continues. If travel is necessary, discuss it with the health authorities so that your follow-up can continue where you are going.
