## From person to person

The virus enters the body through broken skin or through the eyes, nose or mouth. This can happen through contact with:

- the blood or body fluids of a person who is sick with Ebola or has died of it, such as vomit, stools, urine, sweat, saliva or breast milk;
- objects that have touched these fluids, such as clothes, bedding, needles or medical equipment.

## Who is most at risk

- family members and others who care for a sick person;
- health and care workers, when infection-control measures are not strictly followed;
- people who touch the body of someone who has died, for example during funeral or burial rituals.

## What does not spread Ebola

- **The air.** Ebola does not spread like flu or COVID-19. You cannot get it simply by being near someone.
- **Mosquitoes and other insects.** There is no evidence that they spread Ebola.
- **People without symptoms.** A person can pass on the virus only once they have symptoms.

## From animals

Fruit bats are thought to carry these viruses. People can be infected by hunting, handling or eating infected animals, such as bats, monkeys, apes or forest antelopes, especially animals found sick or dead.

## After recovery

The virus can stay for some time in parts of the body such as the testicles, the inside of the eye and the brain. It has been passed on through semen up to 15 months after recovery. WHO recommends that male survivors are offered monthly semen tests and use safer sex practices, including condoms, until two tests in a row are negative. Without testing, they should follow safer sex practices for 12 months.

Survivors whose blood has tested negative do not need to be isolated. They should be treated with respect and compassion.
