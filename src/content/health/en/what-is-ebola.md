## A severe illness caused by a group of viruses

Ebola disease is caused by a group of viruses called orthoebolaviruses. It was first recognised in 1976, in two outbreaks in what are now South Sudan and the Democratic Republic of the Congo. The disease takes its name from the Ebola River, near one of the first affected villages.

Six of these viruses are known. Three of them cause large outbreaks in people:

- **Ebola virus**, which causes Ebola virus disease
- **Sudan virus**, which causes Sudan virus disease
- **Bundibugyo virus**, which causes Bundibugyo virus disease

## How serious is it?

In past outbreaks, on average about half of the people with Ebola disease died. This has varied from 25% to 90% between outbreaks. Early care at a treatment centre, including fluids and treatment of symptoms, improves the chance of survival.

## Where does the virus come from?

Fruit bats are thought to be the natural hosts of these viruses. People can be infected through close contact with the blood or body fluids of infected animals, such as fruit bats, monkeys, apes or forest antelopes. After that, the virus spreads from person to person. Read more in [How Ebola spreads](../transmission/).

## The current outbreak

Since May 2026, the Democratic Republic of the Congo has been responding to an outbreak of Ebola disease caused by Bundibugyo virus. WHO has determined that this outbreak is a public health emergency of international concern. The latest official figures are shown at the top of every page of this site.

There is not yet an approved vaccine or medicine for Bundibugyo virus. Candidates are being tested in research studies. Read more in [Vaccines and treatment](../vaccines-treatment/).
