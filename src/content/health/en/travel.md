## What WHO advises

WHO advises against any restriction of travel to, or trade with, countries affected by the current outbreak. It does not recommend suspending flights, closing borders or refusing entry to travellers from affected countries. Instead, it encourages countries to keep travel and trade open while taking proportionate public health measures, such as checking for cases at borders, airports and other points of entry.

Africa CDC adds that detecting an imported case is not a failure: it shows that surveillance works. Some countries have nevertheless introduced their own restrictions, so check the current rules with your airline or the embassy of the country you are travelling to.

## If you are a contact of a case

If you have been in contact with someone who has Ebola disease, your health will be followed for 21 days. During this time, WHO advises that you avoid travelling or postpone your trip where possible, to protect other people and so that your follow-up continues. If the trip cannot wait, discuss it with the health authorities first, so that your follow-up can continue where you are going. See [If you have been in contact with someone who has Ebola](../contacts/).

## If you travel to or through an affected area

Ebola spreads through direct contact with the blood or body fluids of a sick person or of someone who has died, or with objects soiled with them. To protect yourself:

- avoid physical contact with people who are sick or may have Ebola disease;
- do not touch the body of a person who has died, or their bedding and clothes;
- wash your hands regularly, following the advice of local health authorities;
- do not touch or eat wild animals found dead, and cook meat and other animal products thoroughly.

More in [Protecting yourself and others](../prevention/).

## After you come back

Symptoms can appear up to 21 days after infection. If, during the 21 days after leaving an affected area, you have a fever, great tiredness, muscle pain, headache or a sore throat, contact a health worker or your national health hotline straight away. Tell them where you have been and whether you had contact with a sick person. Only a laboratory test can tell Ebola apart from illnesses such as malaria. See [Symptoms](../symptoms/).

A person cannot pass on Ebola before symptoms start, so a traveller with no symptoms is not a risk to others.

## How an imported case can be contained

Kenya's first case, confirmed on 6 October 2026, was in a person who had travelled from the Democratic Republic of the Congo. All eight cases that followed were among people already known as contacts of that first case, and Africa CDC reported no sustained spread in the community. Early detection and the follow-up of contacts are what keep an imported case from turning into an outbreak.
