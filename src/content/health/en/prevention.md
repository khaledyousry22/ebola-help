## Avoid contact with body fluids

- Avoid physical contact with people who have Ebola or may have it.
- Do not touch the blood, vomit, stools, urine, sweat or other body fluids of a sick person, or items that may have touched them, such as clothes, bedding and needles.
- If someone in your home has symptoms, contact the national health hotline or a health worker. WHO advises that people with Ebola are cared for at a health facility, not at home.

## Funerals and burials

Touching the body of someone who has died of Ebola, for example when washing or preparing the body, is a common way the virus spreads. If someone dies at home and may have had Ebola, do not handle the body. Contact the local health authorities: they can organise a safe and dignified burial that respects the family's wishes.

## Hand washing

Wash your hands regularly, following the advice of your local health authorities.

## Animals and food

- Do not touch or eat animals found sick or dead.
- Wash your hands before and after touching animals or animal products.
- Cook meat and blood thoroughly before eating them.

## If you have been in contact with someone who has Ebola

Contact a health worker or your local health facility. They will follow your health for 21 days after the contact. They may ask you to:

- accept a daily visit from the follow-up team and have your temperature checked;
- report any symptoms as soon as they appear;
- avoid travelling unless you have agreed it with the health authorities;
- have a vaccine if one is available. For Bundibugyo virus, which causes the current outbreak, there is no approved vaccine yet. See [Vaccines and treatment](../vaccines-treatment/).

## Health workers

Health and care workers should always follow standard precautions, and use extra infection-control measures and protective equipment when caring for people with suspected or confirmed Ebola.

## Travel

WHO advises against restrictions on travel to, or trade with, affected countries. People who have been in contact with someone who has Ebola should not travel unless the health authorities agree. If you return from an area with an Ebola outbreak, watch your health for 21 days and seek care immediately if you develop symptoms.
